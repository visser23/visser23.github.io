Two ways to yap

Different situations, same clean output.

⌘N

Notes mode

Press the hotkey from anywhere. A small pill appears at the bottom of your screen. Speak for as long as you want — minutes or hours. When you stop, Yap formats everything into a clean note with an auto-generated title and speaker labels.

Fn

Live insertion

Hold the Fn key and start talking. Yap transcribes, formats, and pastes directly at your cursor — in any app. It detects context (email, Slack, code) and adjusts formatting to match. Auto-latches after 10 seconds.

Record any meeting. From your desk.

Yap uses your Mac's microphone to capture everything in the room — or from your speakers. It doesn't need to join the call or integrate with anything. Just press ⌘N when a meeting starts.

After you stop, Yap identifies individual speakers and labels them by name (inferred from introductions or context). You get a full transcript with clear attribution — no meeting bot, no awkward "an AI is recording this" notifications.

What you get

Format, don't rewrite

Punctuation, capitalisation, and paragraphs. Your words stay exactly as spoken.

Speaker diarisation

Detects who's talking and labels them by name. Works with meetings, interviews, or any multi-person conversation.

Menu bar native

No dock icon, no window clutter. Lives quietly in your menu bar until you need it.

Local-first storage

Notes saved as Markdown files on your machine. No cloud, no sync, no account needed.

Context-aware formatting

In Fn mode, detects whether you're in email, chat, or code and formats accordingly.

Free to use

Yap costs nothing. Bring your own OpenAI API key — you only pay for what you use.

Your data stays yours

Yap processes audio through OpenAI's Whisper and GPT APIs — that's the only time data leaves your machine. Everything else is local.

  • Notes stored as plain Markdown in ~/.yap/
  • API key stored in macOS Keychain
  • No telemetry, no analytics, no accounts
  • No data sent anywhere except OpenAI for transcription
