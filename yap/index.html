How it works

Hold Fn and talk.

Yap types what you say at your cursor, in any app, and checks whether you are in email, chat or code so the formatting fits. Keep going as you let go: words you say just before releasing still make it in.

Or press Cmd+N and keep going.

Record a whole meeting from your Mac's microphone. No bot joins the call and nobody gets an announcement. When you stop, Yap labels each speaker by name and hands you a clean note with a title.

Then ask about your screen.

Press Ctrl+Option+Y and ask about the window you are in, by voice or text. Guide answers as if it wrote the document, can search a folder you share, and never looks at Yap itself.

Questions

What does it cost?

Nothing. Yap is free. Sign in with your ChatGPT Plus or Pro plan (usage counts toward your plan limits), or bring your own OpenAI API key and pay OpenAI directly for what you use.

What leaves my Mac?

With an API key, audio goes to OpenAI for transcription and clean-up. When you sign in with ChatGPT, speech is turned into text on your Mac and only the text goes to OpenAI. Guide sends a picture of the window you ask about, only after you agree and only when you ask. Notes, settings and your key stay on your Mac. There is no telemetry, no analytics and no account.

Do I need to install anything else?

No. Yap is a normal Mac app with everything built in. Open the disk image, drag Yap to Applications and open it.

Which Macs does it run on?

macOS 12 Monterey or later, on Apple Silicon or Intel. There is one download for each.

Why does it ask for permissions?

Microphone to hear you. Accessibility to type at your cursor. Input Monitoring to notice the Fn key. Speech Recognition if you sign in with ChatGPT. Screen Recording only if you use Guide. Setup walks you through each one and updates by itself when you grant it.

Fn opens the emoji picker instead of Yap

In System Settings, Keyboard, set "Press the globe key to" to Do Nothing. macOS then leaves the key to Yap.

Does Guide look at Yap itself?

Never. It ignores its own window and looks at the one you were using.

Is it finished?

No. Yap is alpha software and you may meet rough edges. It is signed and notarised by Apple, so it opens without warnings.
